# Causewave Website Design Audit

Date: 2026-05-26
Scope: Visual and messaging audit of the current static website, with emphasis on why it feels more like a technology consultancy than a CSR/social-impact website.

## Summary Finding

The current website positions Causewave as a technology-led consulting firm first and a CSR/social-impact partner second. The language, visual system, information hierarchy, and service structure repeatedly foreground "technology", "AI", "platforms", "analytics", and "digital transformation" before establishing field credibility, CSR compliance expertise, community outcomes, sectors served, or human impact.

This makes the site feel closer to a SaaS/IT services website than a CSR advisory and implementation partner.

## Evidence From Current Site

- Home hero headline says "Next-Generation CSR Consulting" and the subtitle immediately emphasizes advanced technology, AI-driven communication, and digital infrastructure.
- The first visual impression is an abstract blue wave SVG, not people, field work, beneficiaries, communities, or project evidence.
- Home services are heavily technology-forward: technology-enabled implementation, digital compliance, real-time analytics, CSR technology platform, and AI-powered impact storytelling.
- Services page includes broad IT offerings such as Digital Transformation, Cloud Solutions, Data Analytics, and Cybersecurity. These dilute the CSR positioning and make the company look like a general technology vendor.
- About page repeats the same technology-first framing instead of starting with mission, field experience, public-health credibility, CSR outcomes, and implementation capability.
- Contact and footer trust signals are incomplete: placeholder phone number, generic "India" location, placeholder legal/social links.
- The logo path is broken across pages: HTML references `logo.png`, but the project currently contains `logo_1.png` and `logo_2.png`.
- Mobile hero rendering clips the headline horizontally, which weakens first impression and trust.

## Desired Positioning

Causewave should feel like a credible CSR strategy, compliance, implementation, and impact-reporting partner with field-tested public-health and development-sector experience. Technology should remain present, but as an enabler of transparency, monitoring, reporting, and storytelling rather than the primary identity.

Recommended positioning direction:

> CSR strategy and implementation for companies that want compliant, measurable, community-rooted impact, supported by practical technology where it improves delivery and reporting.

## Priority 1: Hero Repositioning Plan

Goal: Replace the current technology-led first impression with a CSR-first, human-impact-first hero.

### User Feedback Incorporated

- Use the transparent logo assets already present as `logo_1.png` and `logo_2.png`.
- Use phone number `7600596053` wherever contact detail is shown on the prepared home page.
- Do not change the current home-page stats/data display.
- Source a suitable field-impact image from the internet as needed.
- Keep the color palette aligned with the blue/cyan gradient language of the Causewave logo.
- Prepare the home page first, then decide the deeper site direction.

### Current Hero Problem

The current hero says:

- "Next-Generation CSR Consulting"
- "Strategic CSR consulting powered by advanced technology solutions and AI-driven impact communication..."

This leads with innovation language, not CSR trust. A CSR decision-maker should immediately understand:

- What Causewave helps them do.
- Why Causewave is credible.
- What kind of impact or compliance outcome they can expect.
- Whether the firm understands real Indian CSR implementation conditions.

### Proposed Hero Message

Use a CSR-first headline:

> Build CSR programs that are compliant, measurable, and rooted in real community impact.

Supporting copy:

> Causewave helps companies plan, implement, monitor, and communicate CSR initiatives across health, education, livelihoods, sustainability, and community development, combining field experience with practical reporting systems.

Primary CTA:

> Plan Your CSR Program

Secondary CTA:

> View Impact Work

### Proposed Hero Visual Direction

Replace the abstract wave with a human or field-impact visual system. Preferred options:

- Real photograph from public-health, community outreach, vaccination, training, or field implementation work.
- If real photography is unavailable, use a warm editorial-style impact collage: field worker, community meeting, health outreach, education/livelihood activity, and reporting dashboard as a small supporting layer.
- Avoid abstract tech waves, generic dashboard mockups, AI motifs, blue gradient orbs, and SaaS-style illustration.

### Above-The-Fold Trust Signals

After user feedback, keep the existing home-page data display unchanged:

- 50+ Companies Transformed.
- ₹10Cr+ CSR Impact Generated.
- 100% Compliance Success.

The first-viewport trust shift should come from the CSR-first headline, field-impact image, and supporting copy that foregrounds planning, implementation, monitoring, reporting, and public-health/community execution credibility. Additional proof points such as SKOCH/IIM recognition can be considered later, but should not replace the current home stats in this first-page pass.

### Information Hierarchy

Recommended first viewport order:

1. CSR-first headline.
2. Human-impact supporting copy.
3. Two CTAs: plan CSR program, view impact work.
4. Existing home stats row retained unchanged.
5. Human/field visual.

Technology should appear later as:

> Practical systems for monitoring, reporting, and impact communication.

### Design Tone

Move away from:

- SaaS blue gradients.
- Abstract animated SVG wave.
- Generic icon cards.
- "Revolutionize", "next-generation", "AI-powered" as primary language.

Move toward:

- Warm, grounded, documentary feel.
- Human-centered photos or impact imagery.
- More editorial layout, less software-dashboard styling.
- Trust-first typography and restrained CTA language.
- Sector cues: health, education, livelihood, environment, community development.

## Suggested Implementation Sequence

1. Fix the broken logo reference so the brand appears correctly.
2. Rewrite the home hero headline, subtitle, CTAs, and trust row.
3. Replace the abstract hero SVG with a field-impact visual.
4. Adjust hero styling for a warmer CSR look and fix mobile headline overflow.
5. Move technology-heavy language from the first viewport into a later "How We Enable Better Reporting" section.
6. Recheck desktop and mobile screenshots before touching deeper service pages.

## Acceptance Criteria For Priority 1

- First viewport clearly reads as CSR/social-impact consulting, not tech consulting.
- Human/community/field credibility is visible above the fold.
- Technology is present only as a supporting capability.
- Logo loads successfully.
- Hero text does not clip on mobile.
- CTAs match CSR buyer intent.
- The hero introduces at least one credible proof point tied to field work or recognized impact.
